Scleral Lenses: Sharp Vision for Irregular Corneal Shapes

For patients with Keratoconus, post-refractive ectasia, or corneal scarring, standard glasses and soft contact lenses often fail to provide clear vision. Custom gas-permeable scleral lenses offer an innovative optical solution, delivering sharp vision and exceptional comfort.

Medically Reviewed by Dr. Mohamed El-Naggar

Consultant Cornea & Refractive Surgeon | Fellow of the Royal College of Surgeons (FRCS) • Updated September 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Vaulting Design: Scleral lenses completely bridge over distorted corneal tissue, resting gently on the less sensitive sclera.
  • Liquid Reservoir: The space between lens and cornea is filled with sterile saline, smoothing out optical irregularities.
  • Superior Comfort: Eliminates the mechanical friction common with traditional small-diameter rigid gas-permeable (RGP) lenses.
  • Therapeutic Benefits: Constantly hydrates the ocular surface, offering relief for severe Dry Eye Syndrome and Sjögren's Syndrome.

How Scleral Lenses Work

A scleral lens is a large-diameter, gas-permeable contact lens designed to vault completely over the cornea and align with the conjunctiva tissue covering the white sclera of the eye.

Because the lens does not touch the compromised corneal tissue, it creates a smooth optical front surface. The space between the back surface of the lens and the corneal dome is filled with preservative-free saline, which neutralizes irregular corneal astigmatism and corrects visual distortion.

Who Benefits Most from Scleral Lenses?

Scleral lenses are particularly beneficial for individuals with challenging corneal conditions:

  • Corneal Ectatic Disorders: Advanced Keratoconus, Pellucid Marginal Degeneration, and Post-LASIK / PRK Ectasia.
  • Post-Surgical Corneas: Post-Corneal Graft (PKP or DALK) irregular astigmatism that cannot be managed with standard lenses.
  • Corneal Scars & Trauma: Scars resulting from healed corneal ulcers or ocular injuries.
  • Severe Ocular Surface Disease: Refractory Dry Eye, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, and Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD).

The Precision Fitting Process

Achieving ideal visual results and daily comfort requires careful customization:

  1. 3D Profilometry & Tomography: Detailed digital mapping of the corneal and scleral contours.
  2. Diagnostic Fitting: Placing a trial scleral lens and assessing central clearance, limbal clearance, and landing zone alignment using Anterior-Segment OCT.
  3. Custom Fabrication: Ordering specialized gas-permeable lenses with tailored vault heights and toric landing zones.
  4. Insertion & Hygiene Guidance: Hands-on patient instruction regarding sterile saline filling and proper insertion/removal techniques.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do scleral lenses differ from standard soft contact lenses?

Unlike standard soft lenses that rest directly on the delicate cornea, scleral lenses vault entirely over the cornea and align with the white sclera, holding a soothing fluid reservoir that neutralizes optical irregularities.

Are scleral contact lenses comfortable to wear?

Yes. Because scleral lenses rest on the less sensitive sclera and maintain a constant hydrating fluid barrier, they are often substantially more comfortable than traditional rigid gas-permeable (RGP) lenses.
