Understanding Keratoconus

Keratoconus is a progressive eye condition in which the normally round cornea thins and begins to bulge into a cone-like shape, causing distorted vision.

1

Corneal Thinning

Weakening of the structural collagen fibers in the cornea, impacting how light enters and focuses in the eye.

2

Conical Bulging

Gradual transformation of the corneal shape from smooth and spherical to an irregular conical contour.

3

Visual Distortion

Increased irregular astigmatism and blurry vision that cannot be fully corrected with standard eyeglasses.

Warning Signs to Watch For

If you experience any of these symptoms, a detailed Corneal Topography assessment is highly recommended.

Frequent Prescription Changes

Needing to change your eyeglass or contact lens prescription repeatedly within short periods.

Distorted & Double Vision

Experiencing streakiness, ghosting, or multiple images when viewing objects with one eye covered.

Light Sensitivity & Halos

Increased glare, halos around bright light sources, and difficulty driving at night.

Advanced Treatment Options

Treatment plans are customized based on the progression stage, corneal thickness, and patient age.

Corneal Cross-Linking (CXL)

A minimally invasive procedure using UV light and riboflavin to strengthen corneal collagen bonds and halt progression.

Intracorneal Ring Segments (ICRS)

Micro-thin arc inserts placed in the cornea to flatten the cone, reduce astigmatism, and improve vision clarity.

Corneal Transplantation

A surgical procedure reserved for advanced stages to replace damaged corneal tissue with healthy donor tissue.
