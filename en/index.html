Understanding Keratoconus

Your cornea is changing shape.
What does that mean?

Keratoconus is a progressive condition where the cornea—the clear front surface of your eye—thins and gradually bulges outward into a cone-like shape. This distortion causes light entering the eye to scatter unevenly, producing distorted, blurry vision that standard glasses cannot fully correct.

The condition typically begins in late adolescence or early twenties and may progress over 10 to 20 years. The earlier it is detected, the more options you have available. Early stages can often be managed with specialty contact lenses, while more advanced stages benefit from surgical interventions to stabilize the structure and improve functional vision.

The key takeaway: Keratoconus is treatable at every stage. Treatment goals shift depending on severity—from stopping progression to restoring functional acuity—but no stage is without a solution.

Warning Signs

Signs That Point to
Keratoconus

Distorted or Blurred Vision

Straight lines appear wavy or bent. Text and faces remain unclear even with brand-new glasses.

Frequent Prescription Changes

Eyeglass prescriptions change every few months, yet new lenses never provide crisp clarity.

Glare, Halos & Light Sensitivity

Experiencing bright halos around night lights, sensitivity to glare, and difficulty driving after dusk.

Monocular Double Vision

Seeing ghost images, multiple shadows, or double vision out of a single eye when the other is closed.

Chronic Eye Rubbing

Vigorous eye rubbing is both a key symptom and a primary risk factor that accelerates progression.

Early Youth Onset

Keratoconus usually emerges between ages 15–25. Rapid vision changes during youth warrant a corneal scan.

Treatment Options

Dr. Mohamed El-Naggar evaluates every case individually, recommending only treatments tailored to your exact corneal topography and lifestyle requirements.

Corneal Cross-Linking (CXL)

The gold standard for progressive keratoconus. Riboflavin drops combined with UV light strengthen corneal collagen bonds to halt further thinning. A 30–60 minute outpatient procedure designed to preserve existing vision.

Stops Progression · Outpatient

Corneal Rings (ICRS)

Microscopic arc-shaped implants placed into the cornea to reshape and flatten the cone. Improves visual clarity and lens tolerance in moderate cases. Can be combined with CXL for dual benefits.

Improves Vision · Reversible

Corneal Transplant

Reserved for advanced cases with severe scarring or extreme thinning. Dr. El-Naggar performs both partial-thickness (DALK) and full-thickness (PKP) transplants depending on tissue integrity.

Advanced Cases · Long-term Solution

You May Be Suitable If:

  • You have been diagnosed with keratoconus at any stage
  • Your prescription shifts repeatedly despite new glasses
  • You are between the ages of 14 and 55
  • You were told your cornea is thinning or irregular
  • You have a family history of keratoconus and need screening

Honest Facts to Consider:

  • Not every procedure is suitable for every stage of ectasia
  • Cross-Linking halts progression; it does not fully reverse existing scarring
  • Advanced scarring may necessitate corneal graft procedures
  • We will never recommend surgery unless high-resolution scans confirm safety

Everything You Need to Know
About Keratoconus Treatment

Keratoconus rarely leads to total blindness. While it causes progressive distortion that can severely hamper daily tasks if left untreated, modern interventions like Cross-Linking can halt progression early. Timely detection is essential to protect your baseline visual performance.
Anesthetic eye drops ensure the procedure is completely painless. You will recline comfortably while riboflavin drops and UV light are applied for 30–60 minutes. Mild tearing or irritation may occur for 2–3 days post-procedure, managed easily with prescribed comfort drops. Most patients describe it as mild discomfort rather than pain.
Initial light sensitivity and tearing peak over the first 3 to 5 days. Most patients return to desk work or studies within a week. While vision may fluctuate initially, complete visual stabilization takes 3 to 6 months. The stabilizing effect of the procedure on corneal strength is generally permanent.
Standard LASIK and SMILE Pro are contraindicated in untreated keratoconus because removing corneal tissue worsens stability. However, after Cross-Linking, select mild cases may be candidates for specialized surface treatments (like topography-guided PRK). Dr. El-Naggar will evaluate your scans to determine the safest route.
Treatment costs vary depending on whether you require Cross-Linking, ring segment implantation, or corneal grafting. Rather than generalized estimates, we provide a complete transparent breakdown after analyzing your high-resolution Pentacam scan during your initial consultation.
Cross-Linking can be safely performed on patients as young as 14—and occasionally younger if rapid progression is detected. Because keratoconus progresses faster in adolescents, early stabilization is critical to protecting lifelong visual outcome.
